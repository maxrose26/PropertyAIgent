# 033. Retaining and screening verified records when window completeness is unknown

Status: **revised proposal; Phase 1 (below) is implemented offline in the accompanying candidate, Phase 2 is not.** Depends on specification 032 (sections 12-14). Stage 4 remains OPEN. No live execution is authorised by this document.

## 1. Intelligence layer and purpose
Planning Applications (the record layer that feeds every Site). A verified listing record should remain usable and screenable when the *window* it was found in cannot be proved complete, **without** any claim of completeness, absence or coverage.

## 2. Evidence this rests on (and what it does not show)
* 20 October 2026, Bolton RECEIVED 16 October only: one page of 4 verified rows; the recognised `p.pager` element was absent; the criteria echo was accepted; the existing contract correctly refused completeness (`TOTAL_UNKNOWN`). The page body was **not retained**, so no other count or pagination signal can be confirmed or excluded.
* An earlier real Bolton observation (recorded 14 October; a historical single-day window; 8 rows; no pager). Two Bolton observations are consistent with the pager appearing only when results exceed one page: an **observed pattern, not proven council design**.
* Every other retained non-empty accepted window (Bolton 22 and 24, Bury 18, Oldham 14, Tameside 15, Wigan 17, Stockport 49) is multi-page with an explicit total (Bury also has one explicit-zero result, a different signal). **Single-page behaviour has been observed for Bolton only.**
* **Fixtures are reconstructions.** Because the 20 October page was not retained, every single-page test fixture is a *reconstruction* built with the repository's own page generator to reproduce the **parsed shape** (rows, no `p.pager`, accepted echo, unknown total). The multi-page fixtures reproduce the shape retained from the 14 October observation. They are not the original bytes, are labelled as reconstructions in the tests, and prove nothing about how Bolton actually renders any page.

## 3. Principle: two claims that must never be conflated
* **Record claim** - "this reference was listed by the council with these literal fields at this observation time, with this provenance". Verifiable per record.
* **Window claim** - "these are all the records for this window". Needs an explicit source signal (today: `COUNT_RECONCILED`).
A *partial observation* asserts the record claim only. Its single window-level statement is the constant `window_claim = "NONE"`.

## 4. The partial observation (`LISTING_OBSERVATION_V1`, not a weaker receipt)
**Schema (resolving the earlier contradiction).** The kind contains exactly: `kind`, `contract`, `council`, `requested_interval`, `applied_interval_evidence` (method, evidence kind, sha256), `records[]`, `page` (`body_sha256`, `rows`, `page_size`), `observed_at` (UTC), `window_claim` (constant `NONE`), `display_label`, `source_sha256`, `qualification_sha256`, `cleanup`, `accounting_complete`, `outer_supervisor`, `resources`. It contains **no completeness vocabulary at all**: no `completeness`, `enumeration_evidence`, `terminal`, `total`, `first`/`last`, `window`, `capped`, `segments`, `applied_interval`, `coverage` or `watermark` field, and no value `COMPLETE`/`COUNT_RECONCILED`. (The earlier draft listed `completeness = NOT_CLAIMED` and `enumeration_evidence = TOTAL_UNKNOWN` as fields while also saying there was nothing completeness could be read from: that contradiction is removed. "Unknown total" is the *reason the kind exists*, recorded in the run's audit, never a field of the observation.)
Each record carries reference, source key, literal fields, URL, and provenance (page body sha, row ordinal).

## 5. Admission (initial scope: Bolton RECEIVED only, explicit enablement)
Default OFF: a source without an explicit total keeps stopping exactly as before. Admitted only when the contract is explicitly constructed with `partial_observation=True`, the council is in the enabled list (`bolton`) and semantics are RECEIVED, **and** all hold: the existing source binding checks pass (council, semantics, requested and applied interval, no failure, not capped, cleanup closed); the source reports an unknown total with the strict criteria echo and no explicit-zero evidence; **exactly one results page** with no first/last/total/terminal; the page records its **selected page size**; the result count is **non-empty and strictly below the selected page size**; every row passes the existing strict row validation (identity present and unique, date inside the interval, canonical URL); resource, accounting, provenance and criteria-binding checks unchanged; zero redirects and retries. Anything else - a pager that disagrees, a next link, a full page, an echo mismatch, a duplicate reference, a row outside the interval - stops exactly as today. Zero results for Bolton are unobserved and out of scope.
Admission does **not** infer completeness from the short page, from the absent pager or from the matching date criteria.

## 6. Prohibitions (enforced in code and tests)
P1 no completeness claim; P2 no absence inference ("no other applications were received"); P3 no "new application" or "newly submitted" logic - the platform records only **when it first observed** an application, which is not when the council received or published it; P4 no completeness watermark or coverage metric advance; P5 never supersedes or downgrades a `LISTING_RECEIPT_V1`; P6 every window-claim consumer rejects the kind by type (batching, capture resume, `capture_once` even for a COMPLETE-state checkpoint, the coordinator resume path and the database functions all require `LISTING_RECEIPT_V1`).

## 7. Application identity, observation history and conflict handling
* **Identity** is (council, reference). Deduplication happens at the application level; **every observation is preserved** append-only with its UTC `observed_at`, evidence digest and per-record digest. Earlier entries are never altered or removed (explicit invariant checks on every append and link, not assertions). The `change` label compares an entry with the previously **appended** entry (delivery order); chronology is carried by `observed_at`, and `first_observed` is the earliest by time. Evidence is digested in its stored (JSON) form, so an in-memory receipt and its stored copy give one digest. Linking is idempotent and compares on the council's received date only (RECEIVED semantics).
* **First observed versus newly submitted.** `first_observed_at` is the earliest observation timestamp *by this platform*, with the evidence it came from, plus the council's own `received_date` kept separately. There is deliberately no API or field that says "newly submitted".
* **Normal update versus conflicting evidence.** Between consecutive observations of one identity: `UNCHANGED`; `NORMAL_UPDATE` when a descriptive field (proposal, address, application type) changed or a validated date appeared for the first time; `CONFLICT` when the source key changes, the received date changes, a published validated date changes or disappears, or one source key is claimed by another reference. A conflict is flagged and **both sides stay**; nothing is overwritten.
* **Linking a later complete capture.** A separate `links` list relates each earlier observation to the later `LISTING_RECEIPT_V1`: `PRESENT_IN_COMPLETE_CAPTURE`, or `OBSERVED_BUT_ABSENT_FROM_COMPLETE_CAPTURE` (a review flag recorded as conflicting evidence, never a deletion). Neither the observation nor the receipt is replaced; the receipt's records are appended to the history as their own entries.

## 8. Retention
Observations reuse the **existing capture-retention behaviour**: the same durable raw archive on the evidence branch, the same ledger checkpoints, the same sealed run artifacts. **No new duration is invented.** Finding: the repository has *behaviour* but no *written policy*: the evidence-branch archive and ledger rows have no deletion path (effectively indefinite), CI run artifacts are kept for the workflow's configured days, and the only retention statements found elsewhere ("full historic retention" for monitored reports; "log level, retention and access remain rollout prerequisites", specification 021) do not cover captures. **Missing policy, to be decided by the product owner before any deletion capability exists: how long retained captures, observations and their derived screening results are kept, and whether the raw archive and the ledger may be pruned.** The history module offers no way to delete.

## 9. Presentation
Any surface showing observed records uses the label **"Observed applications — window completeness not established"** (constant `OBSERVATION_DISPLAY_LABEL`); `display_label()` returns it for observations and nothing for complete receipts.

## 10. Reuse, and the phase boundary
Reused unchanged: window runner and request boundary, `parse_page`, resource ceilings, the per-row validation (extracted into one shared helper used by both paths), `screen_row` (window-independent), archive-before-checkpoint, queue, publication-before-closure, allowance machinery (no generation-agnostic allowance is introduced). One audit addition: each non-empty results page now records its `page_size` (the explicit-zero page has none and does not need one). Receipts produced after this change carry the extra key, so their digests differ from a pre-change receipt of the same source; stored historical receipts are not recomputed.
* **Phase 1 - implemented offline in this candidate:** the observation kind and admission (`IdoxListingContract.observe`, default OFF), the record-level screening wrapper with provenance (`screen_observation`), the append-only history, classification, first-observed and complete-capture linking (`observation_history`, pure functions), the display constant, the page-size audit field, and the tests. **No mode, registration field, workflow or database function enables it; it cannot run in production.**
* **Phase 2 - NOT implemented, needs separate approval:** the ledger path (the database functions `ingestion_worker_step` currently require `LISTING_RECEIPT_V1` at the capture and batch steps, so persisting observations needs a reviewed database-function change and its own ceremony), the owner/hosted mode and the explicit registration field, persistence of the history, and product surfaces.

## 11. Acceptance criteria
Phase 1 (each is a test in `tests/test_ingestion_listing_observation.py` unless stated):
1. **Schema separation:** the exact key set above; no completeness-vocabulary key anywhere; no `COMPLETE`/`COUNT_RECONCILED` value; the kind is rejected by batching and capture resume.
2. **Admission** from the *reconstructed* 4-row page when enabled; the same input stops with `IDOX_WINDOW_BINDING_OR_COMPLETENESS` under the default contract and under an enabled `project`.
3. **Strictly below the selected page size:** 1, 4 and 9 of 10 admitted; 10 of 10, 0, and 5 of a selected 5 stopped; a missing page size stopped; 4 of a selected 5 admitted.
4. **Anomalies stop:** complete, capped, failed, wrong interval or semantics, zero evidence, broken criteria hash or binding, altered provenance, unclosed cleanup, pager/total/next-link shapes, two pages, redirects, retries, duplicate reference, row outside the interval, non-UTC timestamp.
5. **Scope:** off by default; other councils and VALIDATED semantics refused.
6. **Screening parity:** identical per-record screens for an observation and the same records in a complete receipt; provenance records source kind, `window_claim NONE`, observation digest and `observed_at`; a receipt is refused.
7. **History:** append-only with timestamps; the classification table in section 7 for update/conflict cases; first-observed distinct from received date and out-of-order stamps; no "newly" API; no delete/watermark/coverage/absence names.
8. **Linking:** observations and receipt unchanged; present/absent relations and conflict flag; observations not replaced.
9. **Shared validation:** the complete projection's records equal the observation's for the same rows; the complete receipt now carries `page_size`.
Phase 2 (deferred): database-function change reviewed on a disposable PostgreSQL 17 including rollback; lifecycle with the real memory scope; explicit registration binding validated by the owner; first live use under its own approval; retention policy decision (section 8).
