Status: **specification (merged, #166). Phase B (database), production activation, re-screening, council requests and Stage advancement remain unauthorised.** Specification version `residential-count-v2` (v2 separates proposed and approved counts; v1 was the proposed-only draft). Executable acceptance examples: `specifications/035-residential-count-examples.json`, checked by `tests/test_spec035_residential_count_examples.py` against the pure reference in `tests/spec035_reference.py`. Builds on 004, 024/025 (CountAssessment, subject decomposition), 028 (mandate fit), 029-stage4 (material discovery), 033/034 (observations and Phase 2). Stage 4 remains OPEN.

## 1. Intelligence layer and purpose
Planning Applications and Housing Mix, feeding the Site. The question this answers for every valid application is: **how many homes does it propose, on what evidence, and is that enough to treat it as a scheme?** It answers it once, for the platform, before any buyer is considered.

## 2. Product architecture (the clarification)
Three decisions, in this order, never merged:
1. **Retention (every valid application).** Discovery keeps every valid identity and its evidence (specification 029: the 10-unit threshold is not a discovery filter). Nothing below is a reason to discard or to prune (retention policy remains unresolved; interim rule: preserve everything).
2. **Scheme admission (platform-wide, buyer-independent).** An application is **admitted as a scheme** when a **supported, applicable proposed or approved residential dwelling count is at least the platform admission threshold** (configuration `unit_threshold`, 10 for every council today). Admission is a **size-eligibility** decision; planning status, category and every other admission condition are separate gates and are not decided by a count. Admission triggers ingestion into the scheme layer and deeper assessment (documents, extraction, site linking).
3. **Buyer matching (afterwards).** Mandates, buyer minimum and maximum sizes, tenure and geography (specification 028) apply to admitted schemes only and never influence admission. A scheme that no current buyer wants is still a scheme.
A valid application below the threshold is **retained and not promoted**: it stays an application with its evidence and count state, it is not a scheme, and it is promoted only by section 7. `Council.unit_threshold` is therefore the platform admission threshold, not a buyer parameter; its name stays, its meaning is stated here.

## 3. Count model: separate counts, each with its own basis (never one number)
Each count is a separate **CountAssessment-carried metric** (for the approved basis too) (the existing frozen carrier is reused unchanged: its `metric` is a free string, its precision vocabulary is `EXACT / APPROXIMATE / RANGE / UNKNOWN`). Each is either a number with provenance or `NOT_ESTABLISHED` with a reason code; **absence is never zero and nothing is inferred** ("the existing bungalow" is not "1").
| Metric | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `proposed_total_residential` | Dwellings the application **asks for** in its own scope (new build, conversion output, replacement dwellings). Basis `PROPOSED`. |
| `approved_total_residential` | Dwellings a **permission grants**. Basis `APPROVED`; needs permission evidence (section 3B). |
| `existing_residential` | Dwellings on the application site immediately before, when the text says so. Adjoining or neighbouring stock is not site stock. |
| `lost_residential` | Every existing dwelling that **ceases to exist as that dwelling** because of the application: demolished or removed (`DEMOLITION`), converted away from residential use (`CONVERSION_AWAY`), or replaced or reconfigured (`REPLACEMENT`). Never negative, never inferred from an unnumbered mention. |
| `net_residential` | `proposed_total - lost`, established **only** when both terms are established on the **same basis and scope** (see the definitions below). Can be negative. Recorded for later layers; **not** the admission measure. |
Qualified quantities: wording such as "up to 50" or "not exceeding" records `proposed_total` as a **qualified maximum** (`UP_TO:N`), never as an exact total (reuse `qualified_residential_quantity`). Non-dwelling measures (bedrooms, beds, car-parking spaces, storeys, blocks, buildings, retail or commercial units) are never dwellings; they may be recorded as separate non-dwelling measures. **"Up to N" has no evidenced positive lower bound**: it can be anything from 0 to N, and is treated so everywhere (search, admission, display) unless a **positive minimum is separately supported** by a source and its literal words (for example a decision notice reading "a minimum of 40 and up to 50 dwellings"), in which case the interval is 40 to 50 and the display says "minimum 40, up to 50". An unsupported or malformed minimum is ignored.

**Definitions (fixed).**
* `proposed_total_residential` counts the dwellings the application would **create** in its own scope, including replacement and reconfigured output, and never dwellings that stay unchanged.
* `existing_residential` is the number of dwellings on the application site immediately before, only when the text states it. Adjoining stock is not site stock.
* `lost_residential` is the sum of the three loss reasons above. "No dwellings lost" is recorded as `NONE` only when the text says so; silence is `NOT_ESTABLISHED`, not zero. A conversion that outputs more dwellings than it started with still loses the originals: "12 flats into 24 flats" has lost 12 and proposed 24.
* `net_residential = proposed_total - lost_residential`, with `NONE` counting as 0 and any `NOT_ESTABLISHED` term making the net `NOT_ESTABLISHED`. The default is the proposed-basis net. An **approved-basis net** needs an approved total and permission evidence of the loss. A net is never computed across bases, across scopes, or from a cache. Examples: E14 (-2), E17 (-12), E19 (+12), E47 (-6).

## 3A. Count bases: proposed and approved are different facts
* **Proposed** is what an application asks for; **approved** is what a permission grants. Neither is a version of the other, neither is ranked against the other, and a later proposed revision never overwrites an approval (it sits beside it).
* **Dwelling count, planning status and construction progress are three separate facts.** A refused application keeps its proposed count; a granted permission's count says nothing about whether anything is built; construction progress never changes a count. None of the three is derived from, or overrides, another.
* Mixed-use schemes count **residential dwellings only** (section 3E).

## 3B. The count evidence record
Every count (either basis, every metric) is held as one or more append-only **evidence records**, never as a bare number:
| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `basis`, `scope` | `PROPOSED` / `APPROVED`; the application reference and phase or parcel label (`whole` when the evidence covers the application as a whole) |
| `value`, `precision`, `qualifier` | an exact number, `UP_TO:N` (qualified maximum), approximate or a range; the wording qualifier ("outline", "up to") is preserved |
| `source_kind`, `source_ref`, `locator`, `quote` | where it came from: ledger item or receipt digest, document id and hash, field and character span, the literal words |
| `document_date`, `revision` | the **document's own date** (not the download time) and its revision identifier or content hash |
| `evidence_status` | `SUPPORTED`, `UNSUPPORTED_AS_APPROVED` (an approval claimed without permission evidence), `HISTORICAL` (explicitly superseded), `CONFLICTING` |
| `relations` | explicit relationships stated by supported evidence: `supersedes`, `revises`, `alternative_to`, part-of |
Rules:
* **Approval needs permission evidence.** Only a decision notice or an approved-plans schedule (the permission itself) can establish an approved count. An application form, proposal text, planning statement, officer recommendation or a later document merely citing a permission cannot; such a claim is kept as `UNSUPPORTED_AS_APPROVED` and is never shown as approved.
* **Preserve everything; the latest upload does not win.** Historical and alternative permissions are retained. A later date, a later upload or a larger figure never supersedes an earlier one. Supersession exists only where a supported record explicitly states it about the other ("revision B supersedes A"; "this permission varies permission 1"); an unsupported record cannot supersede a permission. The only other way a relationship is recorded is an owner relationship assessment (next bullet), which is always labelled reviewer-assessed.
* **Owner relationship assessments (D10).** An authenticated owner may record a relationship between records or scopes (supersedes, non-overlapping parts, common development, exhausts the development) as an **assessment**: `actor`, `timestamp`, `rationale`, `citations` and the **exact scope** (the one scope both records are in, or the two or more scopes a scope relation covers) are all required, or it is ignored. It is stored as evidence, never as a document statement, and is labelled **reviewer-assessed** wherever it is used (`relationship_basis = REVIEWER_ASSESSED`, headline suffix "supersession reviewer-assessed", no exact scalar cache exported). It **cannot create permission evidence** (it cannot make an unsupported record approved or let one supersede a permission), **cannot alter planning status**, and **never establishes legal supersession** (`legal_supersession_established` stays false; only a document that says so does). A redundant assessment does not downgrade a document-stated relationship.
* **Resolution per basis and scope.** One record, or agreeing records: `RESOLVED` (or `QUALIFIED_MAXIMUM`). Distinct figures with no explicit relationship: `CONFLICT_UNRESOLVED`, which stays unresolved until their scope or revision relationship is established. Declared separate permissions: `ALTERNATIVE_PERMISSIONS`, shown side by side.
* **Scope and overlap.** Different scopes are not a conflict, but **a phase or parcel label alone never proves two scopes are disjoint**. Unless supported evidence says otherwise the basis is `PER_SCOPE` with `overlap = UNRESOLVED`: each scope stands on its own and **no combined total exists**. Three different statements are kept apart:
  1. **Bounds of the union of the specified scopes**: at least the largest scope and at most all added (49 and 52 give 52 to 101), true however they overlap, and only for **compatible counts** (one basis, one metric: proposed and approved are never added, nor dwellings with bedspaces). This says nothing about the whole scheme.
  2. **A whole-scheme total or lower bound** needs **supported common-development membership**: a relation (a supported source kind and the literal words, or an owner assessment) that these scopes belong to one development. With membership the scheme is **at least** the union's lower bound (52) and has **no evidenced upper bound**.
  3. **A whole-scheme upper bound** exists only with supported evidence that these scopes **exhaust** the development; then the scheme is between 52 and 101. The sum is never treated as the scheme's upper bound without it.
  An **exact sum** (101) exists only with a supported non-overlap relation (a source kind and literal words, or an owner assessment) covering exactly those scopes, and what it is depends on what else is supported:
  * **Non-overlap alone: a combined-scope quantity.** 101 is the count of *exactly these scopes together* (`COMBINED_SCOPES`). The headline says `Proposed (combined scopes): 101`, search judges it exactly but **scope-level** (naming the scopes), and **no scalar cache is offered**. Nothing is said about the development.
  * **Plus supported common-development membership, no exhaustion:** still a combined-scope quantity; the development is **at least** 101 with no evidenced upper bound (search is whole-scheme but only a lower bound: a maximum of 100 cannot match, a maximum of 200 is a possible match). Still no scalar cache.
  * **Plus membership AND exhaustion: an exact whole-development total.** Only then is 101 the development's total: the headline drops the "combined scopes" label (`Proposed: 101`), search is whole-scheme and certain, and an exact scalar cache may be offered, typed `RESOLVED_WHOLE_DEVELOPMENT`. A reviewer-assessed chain is labelled and never cached.
  Without membership, search judges each scope (or the combined scopes) on its own and labels the result **scope-level**, naming the scopes that earn it.
* Evidence is append-only and timestamped (specification 033): "first observed" is not "newly submitted". Source ranking never resolves a disagreement (section 8).

## 3C. User presentation: the headline quantity
Always one clearly labelled headline; a number is never shown without its basis.
| Situation | Headline |
|---|---|
| Approval established | `Approved: 49` prominently; a later proposed revision alongside: `Proposed revision: 52` (or `Proposed (relationship to the approval not established): 52` when no revision relationship is evidenced) |
| Qualified approval | `Approved (outline): up to 50`; with a separately supported minimum `Approved (outline): minimum 40, up to 50` (qualifiers always preserved) |
| Relationship recorded by an owner assessment | the figure is labelled, e.g. `Approved: 52 (supersession reviewer-assessed)`; never presented as document-stated or as legal supersession |
| No established approval | `Proposed: 49`, or `Proposed (outline): up to 50` |
| Several scopes, overlap unresolved | by phase, `Proposed by phase (Phase 1: 49, Phase 2: 52; overlap not established)`; with supported non-overlap, `Proposed (combined scopes): 101 (Phase 1: 49, Phase 2: 52)`; the same figure as `Proposed: 101 (Phase 1: 49, Phase 2: 52)` only with supported common-development membership **and** exhaustion (3B) |
| Relevant count unresolved (conflict or alternative permissions) | **`Unit count under review`** with the competing evidence listed (record, value, source, date, revision); the system never silently picks one |
| Nothing supported | `Unit count not established` |
Planning status and construction progress are shown as their own labelled facts next to the headline, never folded into it.

## 3D. Search
* Three **count bases**: Approved, Proposed, Either. **Either is the discovery default.**
* The user selects a **size range**: a minimum and an optional maximum (default minimum 10, no maximum). The platform admission threshold (section 2) is a minimum only and is a separate, fixed gate.
* **Certainty is judged against the entire selected range.** Each count is the set of values it could really be, as intervals: an exact figure is one value; "up to N" is anything from **0** to N (a positive minimum only if separately supported); conflicting or alternative figures are separate possibilities (one of them is true); several scopes with unresolved overlap are judged as in section 3B: **whole-scheme** (from the union's lower bound, with no upper bound unless the scopes are evidenced to exhaust the development) only with supported common-development membership, otherwise **one scope at a time**, labelled scope-level.
  * **Meets size filter:** *every* possible count lies inside the range (at or above the minimum **and** at or below the maximum).
  * **Possible size match:** some possible counts lie inside and some outside (the uncertainty straddles a bound).
  * **No match:** *every* possible count lies outside. Conflicting figures that are both outside the range are not "possible", even if the range lies between them (9 and 60 against 10-50).
* Examples: 30 meets 10-50; 51 does not; "up to 50" is only a possible match for 10-100 and even for 1-100 (it could be 0) and meets only a range starting at 0, whereas "minimum 40, up to 50" meets 10-100 and 40-50; 49 versus 52 is a possible match for 10-50 and meets 10-100; 9 versus 12 is a possible match for 10+ and meets 5-20; Phases 1 (49) and 2 (52) with no supported membership give a scope-level match (Phase 1 alone for 10-51); with membership the scheme is at least 52 (a possible match for 10-100, no match for a maximum of 51); with membership and exhaustion it is 52 to 101 (a possible match for 10-100, meets 10-101).
* **A size match is not a confirmed quantity, a planning approval or a buyer fit.** The labels are exactly "Meets size filter" and "Possible size match". Every result also states the **basis that caused it** (`matched_by`) and that basis's **quantity status** (`CONFIRMED_QUANTITY`, `QUALIFIED_QUANTITY` or `QUANTITY_UNDER_REVIEW`). A refused application can still meet a size filter on its proposed count; a result matched only by Proposed is never described as approved; mandate or buyer fit is never inferred.
* Results are **deduplicated by scheme identity**: one row per scheme with the best class, the union of causing bases, and every contributing application kept.

## 3E. Mixed use
Only **residential dwellings** count towards any residential metric. Commercial units are recorded separately as `non_residential_units` by kind (retail, office, ...) and are never added: "9 flats and 1 shop" is **9** residential dwellings and does not admit; "10 flats and 2 shops" is 10 and admits; the shops remain on the record.

## 4. Counting rules (R-)
* **R-ATTACH.** A number is a dwelling count only when it is attached to a dwelling noun (dwelling, house, flat, apartment, maisonette, bungalow, townhouse, home, or residential/self-contained/affordable/retirement/extra-care/student unit). It is not a count when it describes bedrooms, storeys, spaces, persons or blocks ("4 no. apartment blocks" counts blocks).
* **R-UNITS.** A bare "units" counts as dwellings only inside an explicitly residential clause and with no other unit type in the clause.
* **R-SUM.** Explicitly joined components of one creation clause, or of separate creation clauses, are **added** ("5 houses and 5 flats" = 10; "9 dwellings and conversion of barn to 2 dwellings" = 11). Components that are not both attached creation counts are never added; a doubtful case is `NOT_ESTABLISHED`, not a guess.
* **R-PRODUCT.** An explicit per-block count multiplied by an explicit block count ("2 blocks of 6 flats each" = 12).
* **R-EXISTING.** A mention is about existing, removed, approved, wider-scheme or parent dwellings when existing/removal/approval/wider wording precedes it in its clause with no creating wording in between, when a transformation ("change of use from/of", "conversion of", "alterations to") precedes it with nothing introducing the new use, or when it is not housing supply (holiday, temporary, caravan, lodge). Such mentions feed `existing`, `lost` or the parent context, never `proposed_total`.
* **R-HOUSING.** Holiday, temporary, caravan/lodge, hotel, serviced and short-let accommodation, and C2 bed counts, are not dwelling supply (`RETAIN_NOT_RESIDENTIAL_SUPPLY`). Specialist housing that is genuinely self-contained dwellings (extra-care apartments) counts; decision D4.
* **R-CONTEXT.** A figure from policy context, a wider allocation, a parent permission or the planning-statement boilerplate (existing `_is_policy_target_context`) is context, never this application's total.
* **R-AMEND.** An amendment, variation or non-material amendment never has a scheme total of its own and never admits alone. A figure it merely mentions is the parent's context (E25-E27). Only a clause that **states the resulting total** ("to increase the number of dwellings from 49 to 52", "to reduce ... from 52 to 49") yields a **revised total**: a `PROPOSED`-basis record in the **parent's scope** with the relation `revises`, shown beside the parent's approval as a proposed revision (E44, E45). It never becomes approved without a new decision notice, never supersedes the parent's approval, and the "from" figure stays context.
* **R-UNUSABLE.** A blank, unknown or truncated proposal establishes nothing (`RETAIN_UNUSABLE_PROPOSAL`).
* **R-BOUND.** Wording analysis is linear in the proposal length (bounded at 8192 characters); a pathological input must not stall a run (the former cubic digit-run stall in the existing `extract_unit_count` is fixed and tested end to end; precondition P0 is met).

## 5. Categorisation and scheme role
Category reuses the existing `classify_application_category` vocabulary (`primary_residential`, `outline_application`, `reserved_matters`, `variation_or_amendment`, `condition_discharge_or_details`, `external_consultation`, `screening_or_scoping_opinion`, `build_to_rent`, `affordable_or_specialist_housing`, `mixed_use`, `non_target_admin_or_minor`, `unknown`). A **scheme role** is added next to it:
| Role | Typical categories | Own proposed total? | Can admit as a scheme? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `NEW_SCHEME` | primary_residential, outline, mixed_use, build_to_rent, affordable_or_specialist | yes, when stated | yes |
| `PHASE_OF_PARENT` | reserved_matters, phases/parcels | its own phase count only | yes, as a phase linked to its parent |
| `AMENDMENT_OF_PARENT` | variation_or_amendment, non-material amendment, s73 | **no scheme total**; a stated resulting total is a proposed revision of the parent's scope (R-AMEND) | never alone; linked to the parent |
| `DISCHARGE_OF_PARENT` | condition_discharge_or_details | **no** | never alone; linked (a progress signal for the parent) |
| `PRE_APPLICATION_SIGNAL` | screening_or_scoping_opinion | **no** | no; watchlist (existing behaviour) |
External consultations are retained as related records of the other council's application (existing `external_consultation`).

## 6. Admission outcomes (the single vocabulary)
| Outcome | When |
|---|---|
| `ADMIT_SCHEME` | a supported, resolved `proposed` or `approved` residential count >= threshold in some scope, scheme-bearing role (size eligibility only) |
| `ADMIT_FOR_ASSESSMENT` | a qualified maximum (proposed or approved, e.g. "outline approval for up to 50") >= threshold, or unresolved conflicting or alternative figures (`COUNT_CONFLICT`) with at least one >= threshold; routed to assessment and never recorded as an exact confirmed total (decision D3) |
| `ROUTE_COUNT_UNCONFIRMED` | clearly residential, no supported count: routed to the **existing** unit-confirmation stage; not a scheme until a supported total exists |
| `RETAIN_BELOW_THRESHOLD` | supported total below threshold: valid, retained, **not promoted** |
| `RETAIN_NOT_RESIDENTIAL_SUPPLY` | no dwellings created (hotel, retail, holiday homes, plant, alterations to existing flats, care beds) |
| `RETAIN_RELATED_TO_PARENT` | amendment or discharge; carries the parent link and parent context count |
| `RETAIN_WATCHLIST` | screening or scoping opinion |
| `RETAIN_UNUSABLE_PROPOSAL` | R-UNUSABLE |
Every outcome carries a fixed reason code, the rule version and the evidence it rests on.

### 6A. Authoritative routing under screening rule v3
Exactly **one** field is authoritative for routing a result, and it is chosen by the result's `rule_version`, never by a consumer's preference. For rule v1 (and the unmerged v2) results the authoritative field is the existing `disposition`; they are historical and are never recomputed or re-routed. For rule **v3** results it is `count_assessment.admission` (the single vocabulary of section 6). A v3 result still carries the v1-shaped `disposition` for compatibility, marked `routing_authority = "count_assessment"`; where the two disagree (for example E17, E44, E47) **the disposition is informational and must not route anything**. Every consumer (the disposition gate in `new_identity.execute`, reports, queues) reads the authority named by the record; a consumer that cannot read it fails closed. During transition no item has two authorities, and no v1 result is promoted or demoted by a v3 rule.

## 7. Promotion of a retained application (no automatic promotion)
A retained application becomes a scheme only through a **new, recorded assessment** (never an edit): (a) new supported evidence raises its own proposed total to the threshold (a form total, a decision notice, a document); (b) a **verified** parent relationship shows it is a phase of an admitted scheme (the existing explicit-wording and citation rules apply; wording alone is an unverified lead, as in the current `EXPLICIT_WORDING_UNVERIFIED_LEAD`); or (c) a Site-layer reconciliation (existing `scheme_reconciliation`) shows several applications are one scheme. A later complete capture or detail page is linked to, never substituted for, the earlier observation (specification 033). Small applications that happen to sit on a large site are **not** summed by admission: aggregation is a Site question.

## 8. Evidence provenance and precedence
Every count carries the fields of section 3B: `source_kind`, `source_ref`, `locator`, the literal `quote`, `document_date`, `revision`, `rule_version` and `observed_at`. Source kinds: `LISTING_PROPOSAL_TEXT`, `DETAIL_PROPOSAL_TEXT`, `APPLICATION_FORM_STRUCTURED`, `DECISION_NOTICE`, `APPROVED_PLANS_SCHEDULE`, `PLANNING_STATEMENT`, `OFFICER_REPORT`, `DOCUMENT_OTHER`.
**Precedence is never a tie-breaker for disagreement.** Disagreeing supported figures in the same basis and scope are `CONFLICT_UNRESOLVED` whichever source kind, date, upload order or magnitude they have (a structured form total does not outrank the proposal text; the proposal text does not outrank a statement). Source kind has exactly three uses:
1. **Admissibility:** only a decision notice or approved-plans schedule can support the approved basis; everything else is `UNSUPPORTED_AS_APPROVED` for that basis.
2. **Citation order when sources agree:** all agreeing records are kept and cited; the display lists the structured form first, then the decision notice or proposal text, then other documents.
3. **Ordering of competing evidence** in the "Unit count under review" list (same order), so the list is stable and reviewable.
Only an **explicit relationship** resolves a disagreement: one stated by a supported record (supersedes, revises, alternative-to, part-of), or an owner relationship assessment (3B, D10), which is labelled reviewer-assessed and never legal supersession. Evidence is append-only with timestamps; "first observed" is not "newly submitted" (specification 033). Policy-context, wider-allocation and parent-permission figures are context, never this application's count (R-CONTEXT).

### 9A. Does a single `estimated_unit_count` have to be replaced?
**Yes, as the source of truth; no, as a column, and not silently.** One integer cannot hold a proposed and an approved figure, several scopes, a conflict, a qualified maximum, a source, a document date or a revision, and the evidence-model examples cannot be expressed in it. It also silently chooses (the largest candidate, or the last write).
* **The existing columns are not repurposed.** `Application.estimated_unit_count` and `SchemeIntelligence.total_units_final` keep their stored values and their current (unqualified, legacy) meaning exactly as they are. Nothing in this work rewrites them, backfills them, or changes what they mean. Existing unqualified consumers (the Explore page, site headline, entity search, buyer matching, dashboard, reports) therefore keep receiving exactly what they receive today; **none receives a newly derived number through an old name**.
* **The read model never returns a bare number.** Every answer carries the basis, the qualifier ("outline", "up to"), the state (`RESOLVED`, `QUALIFIED_MAXIMUM`, `CONFLICT_UNRESOLVED`, `ALTERNATIVE_PERMISSIONS`, `PER_SCOPE`, `NOT_ESTABLISHED`) and the evidence. When a single exact figure is available it may be offered as an **exact scalar cache under a new name** (`headline_scalar`: value, basis, qualifier, state, semantics version `residential-count-v2:headline_exact`), always *next to* those fields, never instead of them; the basis is the headline basis (approved when established; a proposed revision is not swapped in for an approval); there is no scalar at all for a qualified, conflicting, alternative or unresolved-overlap state.
* **Consumers migrate one at a time, explicitly.** Each consumer is moved to the read model in its own reviewed change that shows the label; until then it stays on the legacy column. A compatibility adapter that feeds a legacy consumer from the read model is not provided; if one is ever wanted it must be a typed object that cannot be used as a bare integer, and it needs its own review.
* The legacy values already stored are labelled **legacy-unqualified** in documentation and reports.

## 11. Preservation of historical results
Rule versions are immutable and every stored result keeps the version that made it. No automatic re-screening. A re-assessment under v3 is a **new** ledger item linked to the old one by the append-only history; the old result is never edited, re-identified or deleted. Evidence records are append-only; superseded records stay as `HISTORICAL`. Local recomputation is always labelled "local recomputation", beside the recorded values. Archived Bolton audit and receipt identities are unaffected (specification 033 backward-compatibility tests). Existing `estimated_unit_count` and `total_units_final` values are never rewritten by this work.

(1) All 62 examples reach their expected outcome under the implementation (the gap sets in the test shrink to empty in the implementing change). (2) The evidence model reproduces every hand-written bases, headline and search expectation, including the mutation checks (an unsupported source cannot establish approval; nothing resolves a conflict by date, upload order, record order or size; an unsupported record cannot supersede a permission). ## 13. Decisions
**Decided (D1-D7).**
* **D1** The admission measure is the supported **gross** proposed or approved count; "demolish 14 flats, build 12 houses" admits at 12 with net -2, and a negative net does not stop admission.
* **D2** Below-threshold applications are retained in the ledger and observation history only (no `Application` rows); promotion is by a new recorded assessment (section 7).
* **D3** Qualified maximums and unresolved conflicts or alternatives admit **for assessment**; search treats them by the size-range certainty rules of section 3D ("Meets size filter" or "Possible size match"), never as a confirmed quantity.
* **D4** Self-contained extra-care apartments and build-to-rent homes count as dwellings; care-home beds, student cluster beds, hotel rooms and holiday accommodation do not.
* **D5** Aggregating several small applications on one site is a Site question, not admission.
* **D6** `unit_threshold` is the platform admission threshold (documentation only; no rename).
* **D7** PR 163's v2 is superseded by v3 (its attachment logic is reused); it need not merge.
**Agreed (D8-D11); database design and implementation remain separately reviewable.**
* **D8** `estimated_unit_count` and `total_units_final` stop being the source of truth. Their stored values and meaning are not changed; the read model retains basis and qualifier; an exact scalar cache, if offered, is new-named and carried beside them; no unqualified consumer receives a repurposed number silently (section 9A).
* **D9** Phase A keeps evidence in the ledger only; the additive append-only evidence table is designed and reviewed separately in Phase B1.
* **D10** (agreed, revised) A relationship is established either by an **explicit statement in a supported document** or by an **authenticated owner relationship assessment** recording actor, timestamp, rationale, citations and the exact scope. An assessment is labelled **reviewer-assessed** rather than document-stated; it cannot create permission evidence, alter planning status or silently establish legal supersession. Any wider reviewer mechanism needs its own review.
* **D11** Permission evidence is the decision notice and the approved-plans schedule only; any further source (an s106, a condition schedule that restates the approved quantity) needs its own review.
